Widgets

The panel's cards, pinned to the desktop

Widgets are built from the same section pieces as the menu bar panel and drawn in the same theme. A widget never computes anything: it shows the last frame the app received, says how old it is, and stops claiming the present once the app stops renewing it.

Native

Status at four sizes (small, medium, large, extra large), plus the Server widget for one pinned node and for This Mac. The collector reports five incidents, seen only from Seattle and San Jose.

Terminal

The same widgets in the Terminal theme: the TUI's boxed panels, its big digits and its glyphs, sized down to 11 pt.

Light, and the other themes

Native follows the system appearance, and so do Aurora and Linen. Paper and Dusk are only palettes for the Terminal renderer, and the panel and every widget pick them up.

The Service widget

One service's card, in small and medium, as the Server widget is for a node. The small one is the panel's card condensed: three observers with their latency in place of the three meters, each with a mark per check and its recent samples, a passing one rising as tall as it took and a failing one hanging below the line. The medium one adds how it is reached, where it runs, what it depends on, and where the edge in front answered from. Here the primary entry is failing from two of four observers.

The Map widget

The panel's map across the whole widget, in its view and framing, or the panel's. The small one marks each place and names those that need a look; the medium names every place; the large and extra large name each node beside its place. The geo map's land is a dither of fine dots, fading toward the edges; the virtual one wires the places together west to east.

Every state

The small Status widget in the five states the panel has, plus Not current. That one appears when the app has stopped renewing the widget, for example because it quit. It shows the last verdict as history, never as now.

The small Service widget for a service that is up, affected by a path, failing from some observers, down, not monitored, and not current: marks withheld, numbers gone, the samples hatched.

Monochrome rendering

When another app is in front, macOS draws desktop widgets without their background or color. Every status mark has its own shape, so the widget still says what it said in color.

Staying honest without being live

WidgetKit refreshes a widget only a few dozen times a day, so a widget can't follow the 5-second stream. Instead, the app reloads widgets when the verdict changes and renews them on a slow heartbeat. Each reload hands WidgetKit two entries: the verdict now, and a Not current entry dated just past the next renewal. If the app quits or the Mac sleeps through a renewal, the widget changes by itself at that moment.

6:30 · verdict changed → reloadentry: 5 incidents
7:00 · heartbeat → reloadentry: 5 incidents
7:17 · app quitno reload
7:45 · deadline (heartbeat + 15 min)entry: Not current

Widgets use the app's theme by default. In Edit Widget you choose only what a widget shows, never what it means.
